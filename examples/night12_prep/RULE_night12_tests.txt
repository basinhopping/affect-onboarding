Why. Dr. Luitel asked what the grid searches found about pooling, specifically SD pooling. The screens and grids
answer it on validation only and around other configurations: alone on the 3-block base, SD pooling +0.007 (not
detectable), max +0.001, attention -0.016; in the 3-block screen's random draws SD 0.595 against mean 0.584; on
the 4-block baseline in grid 1, SD pooling -0.008 on average. This test swaps the pooling of the two finalists
and nothing else.

0.607 (families N9B and N9C). No run with another pooling exists for either finalist.

Order.
 1. The pull of night 12: both sweeps' runs and records, the night's logs, smoke runs and checks.
 2. `affect.cli analyze` of each sweep (confidence 0.95, 2,000 draws, seed 20261012): validation only, recorded;
    nothing below depends on it.
 3. `affect.cli compare-runs` of each sweep: each base's 25 runs against the confirmation's runs of the same
    configuration, fold and seed (cnn_3block_confirm_1 and cnn_confirm_3), which should be identical; reported.
 4. The 8 selection records above, then `affect.cli test-report` of each: the test files of exactly these rows.

Unfinished rows. The analysis starts once night 12's queue is empty or its schedule's end step (08:00 Pacific,
2026-10-12) has run. A row whose 25 runs are not all complete then gets no record and no test read before the
meeting of 2026-10-12; its comparison leaves its family (Holm over those that remain) and it is listed as
unfinished. A sweep whose smoke test fails is not run and is listed with its log.

Method: pairing.py (analyses/night9_20261009, the functions of families N9B to N9G), unchanged, with 2,000 draws:
the difference in fold-mean test UA; a bootstrap over seeds and, within each fold, test utterances (the same resample
for both rows), generator seed 20261012, the families computed in the order N12A, N12B; a two-sided p-value (twice
the smaller tail share around zero, at least 1/2,000); Holm's correction within each family; rows paired by seed
number, fold and test utterance.
Amended on 2026-10-12 at 06:44 UTC (23:44 Pacific), still before any file of night 12 had left the node: the method
first said 20,000 draws. The dry run (analyses/night12_20261012/sim_morning) timed pairing.py's per-draw loop at
about 14 ms a draw here, so 20,000 draws for the 6 comparisons would take about half an hour on the morning of the
meeting; 2,000, as in every earlier family, take about 3 minutes. With 3 comparisons a family, 2,000 draws resolve a
