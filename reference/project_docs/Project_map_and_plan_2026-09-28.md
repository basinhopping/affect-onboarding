## What the results say

- Night 7, the CNN's third confirmation (cnn_confirm_3: screen 3's top 19 and the base on seeds 6 to 10): the 19 candidates span 0.6324 to 0.6430 validation UA, the top three within 0.10 points. On test, cnn_final_3 beats cnn_final_2 by 1.17 points [+0.18, +2.22] (p 0.021, C3P, fixed before it ran; Holm p 0.231 inside C3C), and all three finalists beat the base (C3B, Holm p 0.0015): cnn_final_3 +5.39, cnn_final_3b +6.71, cnn_final_3c +5.47. The validation winner again tested last of its three finalists. Happy and excited carry 56 to 69% of each finalist's summed recall gain.
- The split study read like for like (weekly summary, split_sizes.txt): for the baseline, the speaker-independent splits of all five sessions agree within 0.6 points though speaker out trains on 25 to 41% more utterances per fold; pooled random utterances add 4.3 at session out's training size, conversation-grouped folds 0.6, and speaker out with the partner in training scores 0.6 below speaker out; one gender or one speech type in training costs 5.4 to 8.0 points against 3.3 for a random half. The cnn_final_2 minus cnn_final_1 comparison below was computed on night 4, after both test reads.
- The night-3 finalists beat the baseline by 4.2 to 6.0 test points on unseen seeds 6 to 10; cnn_final_2 beats cnn_final_1 by 2.9 [+1.6, +4.2] and shows no detectable difference from cnn_final_1b (+1.5 [-0.1, +3.1]). Most of the gain is happy + excited recall, from 0.335 for the baseline to between 0.469 and 0.507.
- The CNN search has flattened: screen 3, now complete, keeps night 3's best (0.6435 validation UA on seeds 1 to 3, 07c408b90a3a); six of its configurations score above screen 2's best (0.6411) by 0.06 to 0.24 points, inside screen noise, and none has run on new seeds.
- Splits: pooled random utterances inflate most (+4.3 baseline, +4.9 cnn_final_1 over session out); for cnn_final_1, conversation groups also score 2.4 higher. Training size matters most: a quarter, half and three quarters of the training data cost the baseline 4.1, 3.3 and 1.4 points and cnn_final_1 6.7, 4.4 and 2.9. A random validation fifth gives the same test UA as session out (0.5585 against 0.5590). Enrolling a quarter of the test speaker does not detectably help.
- Night 4 (validation, around cnn_final_1): nothing beats cnn_final_1's settings; group normalization -6.7 and -8.1, mixup -1.5 to -2.1, 128 mel bands -2.0; batch norm is as good as anything tried; a gender head does not help.
- Classical: the ComParE SVM is 2.5 points above cnn_final_2 and level with cnn_final_2b on test. Its shuffled-label control read 0.321 where chance is 0.25; night 5 explained it: with class-balanced weights, 20 shuffled-label SVMs (eGeMAPS) average 0.303 test UA (SD 0.025; 0.259 without weights), 7 of the 20 at or above 0.321. The weights send 57% of predictions to angry or sad. So the SVM rows read against about 0.30. Like cnn_final_2c, it normalizes each test speaker with that speaker's own unlabeled audio.
- Night 5, rule 2 on test: cnn_final_2 beats the baseline under 25 of 26 other splits after Holm (+3.1 to +9.0 points, median +5.6) and cnn_final_1 under 22, behind under none. The exception is the script-held-out split (+0.7, not detectable); removing the tested script from training costs every row 3.3 to 7.9 points on the same test utterances (exploratory, added after the test read). Among the three finalists cnn_final_2 tests first under only 1 of 26 splits and last under 13 (2c first under 16, 2b under 9), as on session out, where it tested last of the three.
